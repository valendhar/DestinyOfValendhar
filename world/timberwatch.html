Timberwatch

A half-timbered village behind a wall of logs, keeping watch over the road east.

A timber and border village of German-style half-timbered houses, built behind an oval wall of sharpened logs. Its name comes from the trade it lives on and the job it was built for.

Black and oxblood-red beams over off-white lime plaster. Steep red tile roofs with moss, jettied upper floors on carved brackets, geraniums in the window boxes, firewood stacked under every eave. Every facade has something leaning on it, hanging off it or stacked against it.

Size
Wall about 190 × 170 m
Gates
South, east and north, plus a west postern
Landmarks
Town hall, inn, Elwyn's tower, watchtower
Lives on
Timber, fields and the road

The main street

Up the hill to the square

From the South Gate a cobbled street climbs gently north. It bends a little on the way, so the square reveals itself rather than appearing at the end of a corridor. The forge glows on the left.

The heart

Market square and fountain

Red sandstone slabs around a nine-metre fountain, where a statue of the village founder, a woodsman-knight with an axe and a wolf at his feet, stands above four bronze wolf-head spouts. Six market stalls, a great old linden with the bounty board, and the town hall on the north side.

Captain Roland's post

Town Hall and Watch House

The grandest building in the village: an arcade of four sandstone arches below, two richly carved half-timbered floors above, corner oriels and a slate bell turret. The guard room is at the back.

After dark

Timberwatch at night

Lanterns on wet cobbles, candlelight in small leaded windows, the inn spilling light onto the square, and the top of Elwyn's tower glowing faintly red above the roofs.
